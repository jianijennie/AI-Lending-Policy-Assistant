The retrieval fix, in one number

"Retrieval hit rate" is whether the pipeline pulled the one correct policy chunk into context before the model even started answering. If it didn't, no amount of prompting fixes the answer — so this was the metric worth fixing first.

How we got here

Four rounds. The first three were keyword patches that plateaued around 91% and started smelling like overfitting. The fourth round fixed two structural problems instead, and that's what actually moved the needle.

1

Onboard 3 new lenders 82.3% → 84.0%

Added Angle, Flexi, and Metro to the lender/keyword lists and re-ingested 62 policy chunks. First full run surfaced the obvious gaps: the intent classifier had never seen phrases like "$400k Low Doc" or "under 2 years", so those questions were routed to the wrong policy section entirely.

2

Switch Groq → OpenAI reliability, not accuracy

Groq's free tier hit both its per-minute and per-day token caps mid-run, silently failing 54 of 175 questions — mostly Metro and Flexi, since they queued last. Moved to gpt-4o-mini, which finished all 175 with zero errors at $0.068 total.

3

Two more rounds of targeted keywords 84.0% → 91.4%

Diagnosed specific failing questions one by one and added matching keywords — "headline rate", "mid doc", "bfs private sale", and others. Every addition was validated against all 175 questions for regressions before being kept. It worked, but the fixes were getting narrower and more literal — several were close to copying the test question's exact wording, which wouldn't help with a real broker asking the same thing differently.

4

Fix the architecture, not the wording 91.4% → 98.9%

Found and fixed two structural bugs instead of patching more phrases (see below). Cleaned up 11 of the 13 narrow keyword patches from rounds 1–3 once they were proven unnecessary under the new design. Net result: fewer keywords than before round 3, and a nine-point jump in hit rate.

The two fixes that actually mattered

Both are structural — they fix a mechanism, not a specific question — which is why they should hold up on questions we haven't tested.

A shared word was silently misrouting CFAL questions to Westpac

Bug fix

CFAL's product is called DriveXpress. Westpac's keyword list includes "xpress" — and "DriveXpress" contains that substring. Because the lender checker tested Westpac before CFAL, any question mentioning both "CFAL" and "DriveXpress" got answered from Westpac's policy chunks instead.

Before
Checked lenders in a fixed list order; first substring match won, regardless of which lender was actually named.
After
An explicit lender name ("cfal") always wins over a generic product-keyword match ("xpress").

Recovered 4 questions immediately, and removes an entire class of future collisions between any two lenders' product names.

The topic classifier was too unreliable to use as a hard filter

Design change

The pipeline guesses a question's topic (pricing, fees, eligibility…) from keywords, then used that guess to exclude every chunk tagged with a different topic. But generic finance words — "credit score," "rate," "abn" — tie across topics constantly, so the guess was wrong often enough that it was excluding the correct chunk more than it was helping find it.

Before
Lender + topic guess as one hard filter. Wrong topic guess ⇒ correct chunk excluded outright.
After
Search by lender alone, and by lender + topic guess, then merge both result sets. A wrong guess can only add candidates, never remove the right one.

This one change fixed 11 of the remaining 15 failures — more than every keyword we'd added across three prior rounds combined.

Where each version landed

Answer score is noisier run-to-run than retrieval — gpt-4o-mini's wording varies slightly each call, which can shift a handful of scores either way even with zero pipeline changes. Retrieval hit rate is exact and repeatable, which is why it's the metric we optimized against.

What's still open

Logged in ErrorLog in the evaluation workbook. None of these are retrieval bugs — they're either a genuine ranking edge case or a model-answer-quality ceiling that retrieval tuning can't touch.

Two long-tail ranking misses — BFS-21, FLX-17

Both correct chunks rank 6th–7th out of their lender's ~8–9 chunks by semantic similarity — just outside the top 5 we retrieve. Fixable by retrieving more chunks per question, but that trades context size and cost for two questions; left as a known limit rather than force it.

Metro, Fees, and Settlement score lower — but retrieval isn't the problem

All three already retrieve the correct chunk essentially 100% of the time. The lower scores come from gpt-4o-mini not reproducing every number in Metro's dense rate tables, which is a prompting problem, not a retrieval one.
